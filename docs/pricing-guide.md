# Palate — Pricing Guide

## Philosophy

Palate is sold to the **host** (the venue, sommelier, or event planner), not to individual guests. Pricing should reflect the value the host gets: a better experience for their guests, post-event analytics, and a tool that replaces paper scoring cards and manual tracking.

Palate is event-driven software — it gets used intensely for a few hours, a handful of times a year. Monthly subscriptions don't fit that pattern well; they feel wasteful during the gaps and create unnecessary friction. The model here is built around **event credits and annual licenses** rather than recurring subscriptions. A first-time buyer can try a single event with no commitment. A returning host buys a credit pack and uses events on their own schedule. A high-frequency operator buys an annual license that reads as a tool purchase, not a monthly bill.

The goal is to remove "yet another subscription" from the buyer's objection list while still creating enough pre-commitment that Palate stays top of mind when the next event comes around.

**Goal:** Get venues using it. The free tier is the top of the funnel — 100% of paying clients start here.

## Why Not Per-Attendee Pricing?

Per-attendee pricing (e.g., $2/guest) sounds fair but creates problems:
- **Unpredictable cost** — a host can't finalize event budget until RSVPs close
- **Friction at sign-up** — "how many people are coming?" is a bad first question
- **Penalizes success** — a popular event costs more, which feels wrong

Flat per-event or monthly fees are how the best comparable tools price (Guidebook, Perfect Venue, CellarPass higher tiers). It builds trust.

## Suggested Sales Motion

1. **Offer the free tier first.** Ask if they have an event coming up in the next 30 days. Help them set it up at no cost.
2. **Follow up after the free event.** Send the analytics report they would have gotten on a paid tier. Let the data sell itself — "here's what you would have seen."
3. **Sell a credit pack** once they're ready to pay. The 5-pack is the sweet spot: enough commitment to matter, not so many events it feels like a burden. "Buy 5, use them whenever you want over the next 18 months."
4. **Upsell to Annual License** once they've used most of their credits and it's clear they're running 6+ events per year. The math becomes obvious.
5. **Upsell to Enterprise** when a venue books a large event (200+ guests) or asks about removing Palate branding.

**Top-of-mind between events:** Send a post-event analytics summary after every event (Palate-branded). At ~45 days after their last event, send a light re-engagement email: "Ready to plan the next one?" The product creates the re-engagement — the pricing just needs to stay out of the way.
